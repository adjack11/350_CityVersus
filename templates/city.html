City Overview

Situated along the Falls of the Ohio River in north-central Kentucky, directly across the water from southern Indiana, Louisville serves as the largest city in the state. Its strategic riverside position historically positioned it as a critical transportation, shipping, and logistics crossroad bridging the American Midwest and the Upper South.

Founded in 1778 by George Rogers Clark and named in honor of King Louis XVI of France, the city grew rapidly during the 19th century as a key steamboat port and commercial trade center. Over the decades, it evolved into a hub for manufacturing, spirits, and sports heritage—most notably becoming the home of the world-famous Louisville Slugger baseball bat and Churchill Downs, host of the annual Kentucky Derby since 1875.

The general vibe of Louisville is a blend of traditional Southern hospitality and midwestern grit. Known colloquially as "Bourbon City," it boasts an expansive urban distillery scene, world-class culinary offerings (from local icons like the Hot Brown to high-end dining), leafy Olmsted-designed parks, and distinct historic architecture. This creates a relaxed, approachable, yet creative atmosphere with a strong pride in local sports, arts, and heritage.
